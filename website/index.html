What it does

Everything the couch needs. Nothing it doesn't.

Six things happen when you switch over, and all six reverse when you switch back.

Display, moved properly

The TV becomes your primary display at its own resolution and refresh rate — not a mirrored, letterboxed copy of your monitor. Your desktop layout is photographed first and restored after, so windows come back where you left them.

Sound follows

Audio switches to the television, and back to your headset or speakers on the way out. Steam can be restarted automatically so its menu sounds land on the right device too.

HDR that behaves

HDR turns on when a tagged game starts and off the second it closes — never left on to wash out your desktop. Games are checked against Steam, PCGamingWiki and a bundled list, and you can always override a tag yourself.

Starts when you sit down

Switch a controller on and the session begins by itself; switch it off and you're back at the desktop. Works with Xbox, DualSense and anything Windows sees as a gamepad — including virtual pads from DS4Windows or DSX.

Tuned for the session

Power plan and Windows power mode go to performance, the TV is kept awake, notifications are hushed and the running game gets a priority nudge. Every one of them is recorded first and put back afterwards.

Puts itself away

Displays, audio, HDR, power settings, window positions and the Steam window all return to exactly how you found them. Reverting properly is the whole point — a utility you have to clean up after isn't one.

How it works

Set it up once. Then forget it exists.

The whole point is that you stop thinking about any of this.

Pick your TV

Choose the display and the audio device you want for the couch. Everything else has a sensible default already.

Tick your HDR games

Your library is found automatically and the HDR-capable games are already tagged. Enable them all with one button.

Sit down

Switch a controller on, or click the tray icon. The TV wakes, Big Picture opens full-screen, and the pointer moves out of the taskbar's way.

Get up

Close Big Picture or switch the pad off. Every setting that was changed goes back where it was.

No injection, ever

Games are detected by watching which window is in front — nothing is injected into any process, which is exactly what anti-cheat is built to object to.

Nothing is force-closed

If you use the optional app-closing feature, windows are asked politely with WM_CLOSE — the same as clicking their X. Unsaved work still prompts you.

No account, no telemetry

It doesn't phone home, collect anything, or need you to sign in. The only network traffic is looking up HDR support for your games.

Questions

Reasonable things to ask

Do I need DisplayFusion, SoundVolumeView or anything else?

No. Display switching, audio switching and HDR are all done directly through Windows' own APIs. It's one executable with no dependencies and no installer.

Will this trip anti-cheat?

It shouldn't. Nothing is injected into any game and no game memory is read or written. Running games are detected purely by watching foreground window changes, which is an ordinary, documented Windows notification.

What if a game's HDR support is tagged wrong?

Right-click any game to set the tag yourself. A star marks the ones you've decided on, and your choice always wins over the automatic lookups — which can change as the sources update.

Does it work with Epic, GOG or games I installed myself?

Yes. Steam libraries are found automatically, and anything else can be added by browsing to its folder. HDR detection and game watching work the same either way.

My controller is a DualSense. Will the auto-start work?

Yes. Controllers are detected as HID devices rather than through XInput alone, so PlayStation and Switch pads count — and a permanent virtual pad from DS4Windows or DSX won't confuse it.

What happens if the app crashes mid-session?

Your display and audio settings are Windows settings, so they survive. Switching back manually restores them, and the next launch picks up cleanly. Nothing is left in a state only this app can undo.
